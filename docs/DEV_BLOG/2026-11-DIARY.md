# Development Diary - November 2026

---

## 11/11/2026 -- iOS: the game on an external display, the phone as its trackpad

Until now a monitor or TV on the iPhone simply mirrored the phone: the app declared no scene for
the external display role (ios/project.yml), and SDL's own scene delegate leaves such scenes alone
"so the system can mirror the display" (SDL_uikitappdelegate.m), so a 1080p screen showed the Duo's
19.5:9 picture with bars and nothing was meant for it.

Now (ios/Launcher/GXLauncher, ExternalDisplay.swift; engine side behind Common/GXExternalDisplay.h):

- The app takes the display's scene: from Info.plist up to iOS 26, from a scene accessory on
  iOS 27. The accessory's view controller must be on screen -- registered from a hidden window it
  never got a scene in the Simulator -- so the pad's window sits under SDL's, covered by it.
- SDL's whole UIWindow moves to the display's scene and back. SDL, DXVK and the engine then see a
  window of another size; `Pillarbox_Process_Resize` already re-creates the backbuffer each frame
  for that (it is the rotation path), so the picture is right at once, fitted with bars.
- The Metal view's scale is lowered so the drawable has at most 1920x1080's pixels (Options.ini
  `GXExternalMaxHeight` moves the cap). SDL computed the window's pixel size from the screen's
  scale, not the view's, and DXVK sizes its present region from it: a 4K display would have shown a
  quarter of the picture. Patches/sdl3-ios-metal-view-pixel-size.patch makes SDL report the Metal
  view's own scale (identical when nobody changes it).
- The resolution follows the display at the main menu, by the Options menu's own sequence (not
  saved); in a match it waits. iOS removes the display's scene at every app switch, so the phone's
  resolution comes back only after the display has stayed away 2 s with the app active.
- While the picture is away the game has a pointer (`GXPointerMode`) and the phone's fingers drive
  the launcher's "Mouse and touchpad" touchpad, without its direct menu taps. The game window's
  loss of key status on the way to the display is no longer taken for the app going inactive.
- The HUD's safe insets (the phone's notch) are dropped on the display.

Tested in the iOS 27.1 Simulator with a harness (ios/tests/external-display) built on the game's
patched SDL and launcher library, since DXVK cannot create a device there: hot plug and unplug,
4K at scale 1 and 2 (1920x1080 drawable, SDL agreeing), 4:3, a too-small 720x480, a display on
before start (title card, display resolution at start), an app switch (no resolution change), the
grace period. The decisions are unit tested on the Mac (`swift test`, 21 new tests). Not yet run on
a device with a real display.

## 11/11/2026 -- iOS: hardware keyboard and mouse

What there was: keys already reached the engine (SDL reads `GCKeyboard` with or without focus), but
text did not without a tap on the field, and never with the picture on a display (SDL types only
through its hidden text field). A mouse moved nothing unless the launcher's "Mouse and touchpad" was
on and iOS drew a pointer over the game's view: SDL takes GameController's raw motion only in
relative mode, which needs window focus, and an iPhone does not lock the pointer to an app.

Now (HardwareInput.swift, HardwareInputPlan.swift, include/gx_hardware_input.h): every GCMouse is read
directly -- raw motion scaled to the window, the three buttons merged across several mice, the wheel
with the Magic Keyboard trackpad's stale-axis report dropped -- and drives the game's own pointer,
the touchpad's, on the phone and on a display. A finger on the screen hands back the touch controls.
With a hardware keyboard a focused text field takes typing at once through a responder that shows no
on-screen keyboard, in whichever window has the keyboard. The system pointer is hidden over the game
and the lock requested; the log says whether iOS granted it.

Tested: the decisions with `swift test` (8 new tests); in the iOS 27.1 Simulator the text responder
follows the picture between the game's and the pad's window, and the Simulator's own keyboard
appears to the app as a GCKeyboard. No real mouse or keystrokes could be sent to the Simulator here.

## 11/11/2026 -- iOS: held mouse buttons with AssistiveTouch

First device run (iPhone Duo, 4K display): the mouse works only with AssistiveTouch on, clicks work,
held drags (left for a selection box, right to scroll the map) do not. The log shows why:
GameController reports the mouse's motion, and each button press arrives as a TOUCH on the phone's
trackpad (`pad finger 1..6 down`). A touch tap is a click, so clicks worked; a touch that moves is the
trackpad's "move the pointer" (handleTouchpadEvent's one-finger motion), so a held left button never
held anything, and a right button never reached the game as one.

GXMouseTouch.h now classifies touches while a mouse is connected: a touch that starts within two
seconds of the mouse moving is the mouse's button if the mouse moves while it is down, if it is held
still, or if it is a quick click; it is a finger if it moves on its own. Presses from touches and from
GameController are merged, so whichever the device sends (or both) presses once. The right button
comes from the touch's button mask or GameController. On the phone itself the same applies to the
game's own view. The first presses are logged with their source, and the pad's first touches with
their type and button mask, so the next device log shows what the Duo sends.
